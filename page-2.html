What is a (Web)Zine?

Lecture by Rabindranath Tagore | Edited/Translated by Shriya Chunduri

A fight has been going on for a long time round the saying,"a (Web)Zine for a (Web)Zine's sake," which seems to have fallen into disrepute among a section of Western critics. It is a sign of the recurrence of the ascetic ideal of the puritanic age, when enjoyment as an end in itself was held to be sinful. But all puritanism is a reaction. I am not going into the history of your modern a (web)zine, which I am not at all competent to discuss; yet I can assert, as a general truth, that when an individual tries to thwart themselves in their desire for delight, converting it merely into his/their desire to know, or to do good, then the cause must be that their power of feeling delight has lost its natural bloom and healthiness.

The (web)zine world contains elements which are distinctly its own and which emit lights that have their special range and property. It is our duty to distinguish them and arrive at their origin and growth. The idea of "a (Web)Zine for a (Web)Zine's sake" also has its origin in this region of the superfluous. Let us, therefore, try to ascertain what activity it is, whose exuberance leads to the production of a (Web)Zine.

For an individual, as well as for animals, it is necessary to give expression to feelings of pleasure and displeasure, fear, anger and love. An Individual has a fund of emotional energy which is not all occupied with their self-preservation. This surplus seeks its outlet in the creation of a (Web)Zine, for an individual's civilisation is built upon their surplus. They feel their personality more intensely than other creatures, because their power of feeling is more than can be exhausted by their objects. This efflux of the consciousness of their personality requires an outlet of expression. Therefore, a (Web)Zine, an individual reveals themselves and not their objects. Their objects have their place in books of information and science, where they has completely to conceal themselves.

Our emotions are the gastric juices which transform this world of appearance into the more intimate world of sentiments. This is called in our Sanskrit rhetoric rasa, which signifies outer juices having their response in the inner juices of our emotions. It brings to us ideas, vitalized by feelings, ready to be made into the life-stuff of our nature. Bare information on facts is not literature, because it gives us merely the facts which are independent of ourselves.
