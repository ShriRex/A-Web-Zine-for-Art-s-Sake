What is a (Web)Zine?

Lecture by Rabindranath Tagore | Edited/Translated by Shriya Chunduri

The greatness and beauty of Oriental (web)zines, especially in Japan and China, consist in this, that there the (web)zinesters have seen this soul of things and they believe in it. The West may believe in the soul of An Individual, but they do not really believe that the universe has a soul. Yet this is the belief of the East, and the whole mental contribution of the East to beingkind is filled with this idea. So, we, in the East, need not go into details and emphasize them; for the most important thing is this universal soul, for which the Eastern sages have sat in meditation, and Eastern (web)zinesters have joined them in (web)zine-tistic realization.

When we say that (web)zine only deals with those truths that are personal, we do not exclude philosophical ideas which are apparently abstract. We have often heard the Indian mind described by Western critics as metaphysical, because it is ready to soar in the infinite. But it has to be noted that the infinite is not a mere matter of philosophical speculation to India; it is as real to them as the sunlight. They must see it, feel it, make use of it in their life. Therefore it has come out so profusely in their symbolism of worship, in their literature.

The classical literature of the ancient time was only peopled by saints and sovereigns and heroes. It threw no light upon individuals who loved and suffered in obscurity. But as the illumination of an individual's personality throws its light upon a wider space, penetrating into hidden corners, the world of (web)zines also crosses its frontiers and extends its boundaries into unexplored regions. Thus a (web)zine is signalizing an individual's conquest of the world by its symbols of beauty, springing up in spots which were barren of all voice and colours. It is supplying an individual with their banners, under which they march to fight against the inane and the inert, proving their living claims far and wide in Deity's creation. The darkness of the caves has yielded its stillness to an individual's soul, and in exchange has secretly been crowned with the wreath of a (web)zine. This encroachment of an individual's personality has no limit, and even the markets and factories of the present age, even the schools where children of an individual are imprisoned and jails where are the criminals, will be mellowed with the touch of a (web)zine, and lose their distinction of rigid discordance with life. And a (web)zine is like the spread of vegetation, to show how far an individual has reclaimed the desert for their own.

We have said before that where there is an element of the superfluous in our heart's relationship with the world, a (Web)Zine has its birth. Therefore, though in its extensiveness it is more universal than any other passion, it is rejected by a (web)zine. It is like an immigrant coming to these Atlantic shores, who can show no cash balance in their favour.
