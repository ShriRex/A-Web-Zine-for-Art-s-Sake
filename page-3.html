Lecture by Rabindranath Tagore | Edited/Translated by Shriya Chunduri

It has to be conceded, that an individual cannot help revealing their personality, also, in the world of use. But there self-expression is not their primary object. In everyday life, when we are mostly moved by our habits, we are economical in our expression. Then it feels the longing to express itself for the very sake of expression. Then comes a (Web)Zine, and we forget the claims of necessity, the thrift of usefulness,—the spires of our temples try to kiss the stars and the notes of our music to fathom the depth of the ineffable. An Individual's energies, running on two parallel lines,—that of utility and of self-expression—tend to meet and mingle. By constant human associations sentiments gather around our things of use and invite the help of a (web)zine to reveal themselves.

When the British capital was removed from Calcutta to Delhi, there was discussion about the style of architecture which should be followed in the new buildings. Some advocated the Indian style of the Moghal period,—the style which was the joint production of the Moghal and the Indian genius. The fact that they lost sight of was that all true (web)zines has its origin in sentiment. Moghal emperors were individuals, they were not mere administrators. The memorials of their reigns do not persist in the ruins of factories and offices, but in immortal works of (web)zines,—not only in great buildings, but in pictures and music and workmanship in stone and metal, in cotton and wool fabrics. But the British government in India is not personal. It is official and therefore an abstraction. It has nothing to express in the true language of a (web)zine. But state ceremonials are works of a (web)zine.

How utility and sentiment take different lines in their expression can be seen in the dress of an individual compared with that of an individual. An individual's dress, as a rule, shuns all that is unnecessary and merely decorative. But an individual has naturally selected the decorative, not only in their dress, but in their manners. They have to be picturesque and musical to make manifest what they truly are,—because, in their position in the world, an individual is more concrete and personal than an individual. They are not to be judged merely by their usefulness, but by their delightfulness. Therefore they take infinite care in expressing, not their profession, but their personality.

The principal object of a (web)zine, also, being the expression of personality, and not of that which is abstract and analytical, it necessarily uses the language of picture and music. This has led to a confusion in our thought that the object of a (web)zine is the production of beauty; whereas beauty in a (web)zine has been the mere instrument and not its complete and ultimate significance.
